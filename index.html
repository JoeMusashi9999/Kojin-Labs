01 / THE PROBLEM SPACESTART WITH PEOPLE

What’s in the air
we don’t see?

Indoor air quality is a systems problem. Building materials, ventilation, everyday activities, and maintenance decisions all shape exposure. Our starting question is where a practical intervention could make the greatest difference.

01

Sources in everyday spaces

Pressed-wood furniture, adhesives, building products, and combustion can introduce formaldehyde indoors. Temperature, humidity, and ventilation affect the conditions we need to study. [1]

02

A gas-phase challenge

Particle capture and gas removal are different tasks. Our project explores media designed for aldehydes, alongside source reduction and ventilation—not as a substitute for them. [2]

03

Who can act on exposure?

Residents may have limited control over materials or ventilation. We want to understand how cost, building operations, and access to reliable information influence the options available.

DESIGN QUESTION

Can targeted gas treatment work without making ventilation harder?

Removal alone is not enough. A useful filter also needs acceptable resistance to airflow, a measurable service life, and a safe replacement process.

AAmine-functionalized porous mediaPrimary candidate · chemical capture+

Investigate an electrospun polymer support carrying amine-functionalized metal-organic frameworks (MOFs) or related sorbents to capture aldehydes through chemical interactions.

Questions to resolve: Binding reversibility in humid air, capacity at realistic concentrations, material stability, particle shedding, and fabrication cost. Permanent capture is a hypothesis to test.

BBase-mediated chemical conversionAlternative candidate · reaction chemistry+

Explore whether a supported basic medium can convert formaldehyde through a Cannizzaro-type pathway under relevant operating conditions.

Questions to resolve: Reaction rate, water requirements, reagent consumption, containment, and methanol/formate products. This pathway cannot be assumed to provide continuous, unlimited treatment.

CCatalytic oxidationAlternative candidate · pollutant conversion+

Assess catalytic materials that could promote oxidation of aldehydes. Photocatalytic and non-photocatalytic routes require different operating conditions.

Questions to resolve: Conversion at low concentrations, required light or energy input, incomplete-reaction products, humidity effects, and catalyst deactivation. Lifetime and safety must be measured.

03 / GRAND CHALLENGESDISCOVER BEFORE DESIGNING

A technical question.
A human-centered process.

PUBP 1142 asks teams to understand complex, open-ended societal problems through collaboration and bottom-up research. Our fall work connects the chemistry of indoor air with the experiences of occupants and the constraints of building operators.

CHECKPOINT 01

Build the foundation

Define why this problem matters to our team. Establish shared responsibilities and investigate the exposure settings where our assumptions need the most scrutiny.

Team foundation · problem motivation

CHECKPOINT 02

Listen & map the system

Plan interviews with occupants, facilities teams, exposure specialists, and filtration experts. Use primary and secondary research to develop empathy and system maps.

Stakeholder research · collaboration

CHECKPOINT 03

Show what we learned

Present evidence, uncertainties, and the less obvious features of the problem. Carry a grounded understanding into future solution development.

Problem-space synthesis · reflection

PLANNED STAKEHOLDER RESEARCH

The right questions
need different perspectives.

These are outreach priorities from our proposal, not confirmed partnerships or completed interviews.

Occupants & housing communities

What concerns do people notice? Who controls ventilation and maintenance? How would an intervention affect affordability and trust?

Facilities & HVAC professionals

What are the airflow, installation, maintenance, and procurement constraints? What evidence would be needed before a pilot?

Health, exposure & policy specialists

Which measurement methods and exposure benchmarks fit the setting? How should uncertainty and potential health benefits be communicated?

Materials & filtration researchers

Which chemistries merit comparison? What is already commercially available? Where are stability, cost, and scale-up likely to limit a concept?

LOOKING AHEAD

From discovery to a controlled proof of concept.

After reviewing the problem evidence, select candidate media for supervised bench evaluation. Any future campus pilot would follow safety review, facilities approval, and successful laboratory validation.
